Customer Health Score Template: A Step-by-Step Framework for CS Teams

Build a scoring system your entire team trusts — in 30 minutes, not 3 months.

A health score is only useful if your team trusts it.

And most customer health scores fail not because the data is wrong, but because the framework behind them is either too vague, too complicated, or too disconnected from what CSMs actually see in their accounts every day.

This guide gives you a complete customer health score template you can implement in a single afternoon. We will walk through the five dimensions that matter, how to weight them, what thresholds to set, and how to roll this out to your team so that every CSM is scoring accounts the same way.

If you have tried health scoring before and given up because it felt like a science project, this template is built for you. No data science degree required. No six-month implementation. Just a structured framework that predicts churn and surfaces expansion opportunities — starting this week.

What Is a Customer Health Score?

A customer health score is a composite metric that tells you how likely a customer is to renew, expand, or churn. It aggregates signals from product usage, engagement, support, satisfaction, and contract data into a single score that your team can act on.

Think of it as a credit score for your customer relationships. A high score means the account is healthy, engaged, and likely to renew. A low score means something is wrong and your team needs to intervene before it is too late.

Why It Matters

Without a health score, your team is doing risk assessment by vibes. Each CSM has their own mental model of what "healthy" looks like, which means:

  • Inconsistent prioritization. One CSM flags an account as at-risk because usage dropped. Another misses the same signal because they were focused on NPS scores. There is no shared language for risk.
  • Late detection. By the time a CSM recognizes churn risk through ad hoc observation, the customer has already made their decision. Health scores surface risk weeks earlier by tracking leading indicators.
  • Missed expansion. Healthy accounts with high usage, strong engagement, and upcoming renewals are your best expansion candidates. Without scoring, these opportunities get buried under firefighting.

Common Mistakes

Before we get into the template, here are three mistakes that kill most health score initiatives:

  1. Too many dimensions. Teams try to track 15 or more signals and the framework collapses under its own weight. Five dimensions is the sweet spot for most B2B SaaS companies.
  2. No clear thresholds. A score of 72 is meaningless if your team does not know whether that is good, bad, or somewhere in between. You need explicit green, yellow, and red thresholds that everyone understands.
  3. Set it and forget it. Health scores are not a one-time project. Thresholds need calibration, weights need adjustment, and your team needs to review scores weekly to build trust in the system.

The 5 Dimensions of a Customer Health Score

This template uses five dimensions, each weighted by how predictive it is of renewal or churn. The weights below are starting points based on patterns across B2B SaaS companies managing 100 or more accounts. Adjust them based on your own data once you have a few months of scoring history.

1. Product Usage (Weight: 30%)

Product usage is the strongest leading indicator of account health. A customer who is not using your product is a customer who will not renew — no matter how many QBRs you run or how friendly the champion is.

Signals to track:

  • Daily Active Users (DAU) as a percentage of licensed seats. This is the most important single metric in your health score.
  • Feature adoption rate. Are customers using the features that drive the most value, or are they stuck on basic functionality?
  • Login frequency. How often are users logging in over a rolling 30-day window? Declining logins are an early warning signal.
Signal Healthy Watch At Risk
Active seats > 80% 40 – 80% < 40%
Feature adoption > 60% of core features 30 – 60% < 30%
Login frequency Daily or near-daily Weekly Less than weekly

Why 30%: Usage is the most predictive dimension across virtually every B2B SaaS company. If customers are not using the product, nothing else matters. Weight it highest.

2. Engagement (Weight: 25%)

Engagement measures the strength of your relationship with the account. High usage with low engagement is a red flag — it means the product is sticky but the relationship is not, which makes you vulnerable when a competitor comes knocking.

Signals to track:

  • Meeting attendance. Are stakeholders showing up to QBRs, check-ins, and training sessions? Declining attendance is a relationship warning sign.
  • Email response rate. How quickly and consistently do contacts respond to your CSM's outreach? Slow replies and ghosting often precede churn.
  • Champion activity. Is your primary champion active, responsive, and still in role? A departed or disengaged champion is one of the strongest churn predictors.
Signal Healthy Watch At Risk
Meeting attendance Regular, on schedule Missed 1+ in 60d No meetings in 30d
Email response time < 24 hours 24 – 72 hours > 72 hours or no reply
Champion status Active, engaged Quiet, less responsive Departed or unresponsive

Why 25%: Engagement is the second strongest predictor of renewal. A strong relationship gives you time to fix usage issues. A weak relationship means you will not even get the chance.

3. Support Trends (Weight: 15%)

Support data is a lagging indicator — by the time a customer is filing tickets, they have already hit a problem. But the pattern of support interactions tells you a lot about trajectory. A sudden spike in ticket volume, especially high-severity tickets, is a strong churn signal.

Signals to track:

  • Ticket volume (rolling 30 days). More tickets means more friction. Track the trend, not just the absolute number.
  • Ticket severity distribution. A few low-severity tickets is normal. Multiple P1s in a month is a fire alarm.
  • Average resolution time. Are you solving problems quickly, or are customers waiting days for a fix? Slow resolution erodes trust.
Signal Healthy Watch At Risk
Tickets / month ≤ 2 3 – 5 > 5
P1 tickets None 1 in 30d 2+ in 30d
Resolution time < 24 hours 24 – 72 hours > 72 hours

Why 15%: Support trends are important but less predictive than usage and engagement. High ticket volume sometimes indicates high adoption, not dissatisfaction. Use it as a modifier, not a primary signal.

4. NPS / CSAT (Weight: 15%)

Satisfaction scores are the most direct measure of how customers feel about your product and team. The challenge is that survey response rates are often low, which makes the data incomplete. Weight this dimension equally with Support Trends as a secondary signal.

Signals to track:

  • NPS score. Promoters (9-10) are your expansion candidates. Detractors (0-6) are your churn risks. Track the trend across survey periods.
  • CSAT score. Post-interaction CSAT gives you more frequent data points than NPS. Look for sustained dips below 4.0.
  • Survey response rate. Low response rates are a signal in themselves. Customers who stop responding to surveys are often already disengaged.
Signal Healthy Watch At Risk
NPS score ≥ 40 10 – 39 < 10
CSAT score ≥ 4.5 3.5 – 4.4 < 3.5
Response rate > 50% 20 – 50% < 20%

Why 15%: NPS and CSAT are useful but infrequent and often incomplete. They work best as a confirming signal — if usage and engagement are down AND NPS just dropped 20 points, you have a serious problem.

5. Contract Status (Weight: 15%)

Contract data tells you about timing and trajectory. An account that is 120 days from renewal with flat contract value is in a very different position than one that is 20 days out with downsell signals. This dimension helps you prioritize based on urgency.

Signals to track:

  • Days to renewal. Accounts inside 30 days need immediate attention. Accounts inside 90 days need a renewal plan. Beyond 90 days, you have time to course-correct.
  • Expansion discussions. Active upsell or cross-sell conversations are a strong positive signal. No expansion activity after 6+ months is neutral to negative.
  • Contract value trend. Is the account growing, flat, or shrinking? A pattern of downsells is a churn precursor.
Signal Healthy Watch At Risk
Days to renewal > 90 days 30 – 90 days < 30 days
Expansion activity Active discussions No activity Downsell signals
Contract value Growing Flat Declining

Why 15%: Contract status is about timing, not prediction. It amplifies the urgency of signals from other dimensions. A red account with 20 days to renewal is a much higher priority than a red account with 180 days.

How to Implement This Template

You have the framework. Now here is how to roll it out so your team actually uses it.

1
Audit Your Data Sources

Before you start scoring, map where each signal lives. Product usage is probably in your analytics tool (Looker, Amplitude, Mixpanel). Engagement data is in your CRM and email. Support tickets are in Zendesk or Intercom. NPS is in Delighted or a survey tool. Contract data is in Salesforce.

For each signal, ask: Can we pull this data reliably? How often is it updated? If a signal is not accessible or requires manual entry, deprioritize it or remove it from the framework. A health score that depends on data your team has to manually enter will not get maintained.

2
Map Signals to Dimensions

Using the five dimensions above as a starting point, assign your actual signals to each category. You may find that some signals do not fit neatly — that is fine. The goal is coverage, not perfection. Each dimension should have 2 to 4 signals. More than that creates complexity without adding predictive value.

3
Set Thresholds With Your Team

Do not set thresholds in a vacuum. Bring your CSMs into the conversation. Pull up 10 accounts your team considers "healthy" and 10 they consider "at risk." Look at the actual data for those accounts and let the patterns guide your green, yellow, and red cutoffs. This calibration exercise takes about 30 minutes and is the single most important step in building trust in the system.

4
Review Weekly and Adjust

Set a recurring weekly review where the team looks at health scores, discusses accounts that changed status, and flags any scores that feel wrong. Use these flags to refine thresholds over time. After 4 to 6 weeks of weekly reviews, your team will trust the scores and start using them proactively instead of reactively.
